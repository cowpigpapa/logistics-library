## Hapag-Lloyd
6. **HL KR import: free time runs from vessel arrival date/time to empty return at the designated place/terminal. 40ft first 5 days KRW 30,000.**
   https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/detention_demurrage/South_Korea_demurrage_detention_import_Aug2025.pdf
   > "Free time for Carrier's Own Container (COC) for all types of container commences from the date/ time of vessel arrival and ends at return of empty unit at the designated place/ terminal." ; 40' "Freetime 10 … 5 KRW 30,000 … 5 KRW 50,000 … KRW 90,000"
   The tariff does not say whether the arrival date is day 1 or whether 24-hour periods are used. The doc states this as an assumption and an open question.
7. **HL KR export: from 00:01 on the first calendar day after pick-up to the FCL cut-off date, 14 days (SOC 10).**
   https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/detention_demurrage/South_Korea_demurrage_detention_export__Jun2026.pdf
   > "commence at 00:01 on the 1st calendar day after pick-up at Hapag Lloyd's designated place of interchange and ends at FCL Cut Off date."
8. **HL US guide: customs hold days are excluded, and holidays and shutout days inside free time extend it.**
   https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/detention_demurrage/Detention_and_Demurrage_Guide_USA_May_2026.pdf
   > "When a container is put on customs hold for no fault of the customer, Hapag-Lloyd stops the Line Demurrage/Detention clock and restarts it when the hold is released." ; "Both bank holidays and shutout days are excluded from free time calculations. If a bank holiday or shutout day occurs during the free time period, freedays will be extended by the corresponding number of days." (The guide also uses the term "Last Free Day": "customs release must follow before the Last Free Day at the inland terminal.")

## FIATA (2018 best practice guide)
10. **Negotiation points.**
    https://fiata.cdn.prismic.io/fiata/a48563de-3aaf-455b-ac6d-e87635ef7a6c_FIATA_World_Congress_2018_-_Presentation_New_Working_Group_Sea_-Best_Practice_Guide_on_demurrage_and_detention-.pdf (p.3–6)
    > "negotiate terms to extend free time for a period that is equal to the duration of the inability of: the terminals to release a container for import or receive a container for export. the depots to receive an empty container."
    > "negotiate limits to the accrual of demurrage or detention charges to a maximum, ideally the value of the purchase price of a new container."
    > (government delays) "The merchant should only be charged for 'compensation to the shipping line for the use of the container'. The second element related to 'encouragement …' should be waived."
    > "for export cargo, the demurrage time is the period from gate-in of the full container into the terminal until the announced closing date for the delivery of the loaded container to the terminal."
    > "levying demurrage charges within a week of the release of the container from the terminal." / forwarders "receive invoices for demurrage charges weeks after the actual pick up … may have problems to collect"
    > (early pick-up for a container number) "the merchant may be forced to pick up, load and deliver the loaded container well before the physical closing"

## Calculations (examples)
- Rule A: day 1 = 12/18 → day 10 = 12/27. Return 12/30: 12/18–12/30 = 13 days, 3 over × 30,000 = 90,000원.
- Rule B (arrival day = day 1): day 1 = 12/17 → day 10 = 12/26. Return 12/30: 14 days, 4 over × 30,000 = 120,000원 (HL 40ft first period).
- Export, Maersk: pick-up 12/1 → loading 12/16 inclusive = 16 days (2 over). Delay to 12/18 = 18 days (4 over). Earliest free pick-up 12/3 (12/3–12/16 = 14).
- Export, HL: pick-up 12/1 → counting from 12/2 to cut-off 12/14 = 13 days (free). Earliest free pick-up 11/30 (12/1–12/14 = 14).
